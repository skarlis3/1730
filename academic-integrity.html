Policies

Academic honesty, plagiarism & generative AI

Academic honesty is highly valued at Macomb. Your work should show your own ideas and your own words. If you use someone else's words, ideas, or work — no matter where it came from — you need to say so. That means citing your sources and being clear about exactly what came from you and what came from someone else. This applies to everything: books, websites, videos, conversations, images, and even things shared in public spaces or meetings.

At a glance

  • Do your own work.
  • Cite your sources.
  • Don't copy off the internet or GenAI.
  • Don't reuse work without permission.
  • Don't fabricate or misrepresent research. This includes inventing sources, quotes, paraphrases, or data.

If you're ever unsure about what's okay, just ask. I'm here to support you — not to catch you doing something wrong. Still, academic dishonesty is serious and can result in a failing grade on the assignment or in the course.

Plagiarism & academic integrity policy

Serious or unresolved instances of academic dishonesty (including plagiarism or fabrication) may result in a lowered grade, a zero on the assignment with no opportunity for resubmission, or failure in the course, depending on the degree and type of dishonesty. An academic integrity report may also be filed with the college.

Academic dishonesty may include, but is not limited to:

  • Having someone complete an assignment for you
  • Having someone make extensive revisions or rewrites to your paper
  • Copying work or ideas from another student
  • Copying or purchasing assignments or text from the internet
  • Using outside information without proper citation
  • Fabricating research — such as inventing sources, quotes, paraphrases, data, or misrepresenting what a source says
  • Reusing work from another class without permission
  • Using AI in inappropriate or dishonest ways (see the AI policy below)
  • Using information without attributing it to the source, that is, failing to cite sources

We'll go over this more in class, but the basic principle is simple: don't copy ideas or information from others without crediting where they came from.

Generative AI policy

Do not use AI for:

  • Replacing your thinking on an assignment, or having AI complete parts or all of an assignment for you. Come up with your own ideas, think through the analysis, and do your own work.
  • Skipping the readings. For the love of everything good in this world and my sanity, please do not have AI “do the readings” for you. Getting a summary first so you know what you're walking into is fine. Getting a summary to replace the reading is not.
    • Literature isn't read just to get an overview of the plot. The prose, dialog, imagery, and everything else are just as important as what happens. Also, in-person literature classes can be actually fun and engaging, but they heavily rely on everyone actually reading the readings.

AI can be used for:

  • Cleaning up and proofreading your paper after you write a “messy first draft.” You must keep an original draft of your paper if you choose to use AI for revision or proofreading help. As long as the original messy draft was yours, ideas and all, I'm open to students using AI to help with the revision process.
  • Help with time management or studying. For example: reviewing texts before the exam or having it quiz you on your ideas about each text, helping you break down tasks or projects into smaller steps, and other student support activities. There are ways AI tools can be useful. Just make sure it's not ever being used in a way that is replacing the point of an assignment or the learning.
