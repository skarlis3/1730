Final Project

Checklist

Finding and Choosing Reviews

  • Three reviews of the reading you chose
  • One professional review
  • One non-professional review
  • A third review from a different site or publication than the first two
  • No study guide sites (such as SparkNotes or Studocu) or AI summaries

Final Project Check-in

  • The reading you chose
  • At least three reviews
  • For each review: the title, the author (for social media, the user name is fine), a working link, and whether it is a professional or non-professional review
  • For each review: a few bullet points on the specific points the author/content creator makes about the text

The Analysis

  • 3–5 specific points for each review
  • Quotes from the text that support or push back against each point
  • A small blurb for each quote explaining whether it supports the point or pushes back against it, and why

Your Take

  • One of the two options: your take on the reviews, or your own review
  • A minimum of 400 words

The Presentation

  • The name of your chosen text
  • A title for the presentation
  • The original reviews: printed articles (stapled if longer), and for videos, a screenshot, a QR code, and the printed link
  • The points the reviewers make
  • Quotes from the text that support or push back against each point
  • Your analysis of each quote
  • Your Take
  • Freestanding: a tri-fold posterboard or something else that stands up on its own, not a flat poster for the wall or a presentation that lies flat on a table
  • Can be viewed by multiple students at a time
  • Content typed up and printed out, not handwritten
  • Laid out in a way that is easy to follow
  • Design choices that fit the reading
  • If you work with classmates: it’s clear whose reviews and analysis are whose, and you don’t use the same review as a classmate

Presentation Day

  • Attend class on presentation day, with your display
