Final Project

The Presentation

Your presentation will display the reviews, specific connections to the text, and your analysis of those connections as they relate to the reviews.

With each review, lay out the points its author or creator makes. Then connect specific quotes from the text that support or argue against those points. Display each quote with a small blurb explaining whether it supports the point or not, and why.

Your presentation needs to be freestanding, polished, and well organized. You aren’t required to use a tri-fold poster if you have another idea for how to display your work. You could build something or put something else together, as long as it looks polished and/or themed to the text.

Optional

For the design of your display, you may also choose to work with other students in the class to create a larger themed display. Each person will still be graded individually for all of their content, so make sure it’s clear whose reviews and analysis are whose.

Required Content

  • The name of your chosen text
  • The original reviews. See Including the Original Reviews below for more information.
  • The points the reviewers make
  • Specific moments from the text that support or push back against each point (i.e. quote the novel or short story)
  • Your analysis
    • i.e. your explanation of why that moment from the text either supports or pushes against the review.
  • Your Take

The presentation must also…

  • be freestanding.
    • You can use a tri-fold posterboard or build something else that can stand up on its own to be displayed during class.
    • Do not create a presentation on a flat poster that needs to be attached to the wall or a presentation that would lay flat on a table.
  • be designed to be viewed by multiple students at a time.
    • The entire presentation cannot be a booklet that only one person can flip through at a time or on a laptop. But you could include a booklet or technology as part of your presentation.
  • include a title for the presentation.
    • This can just be the title of the text or you can be more creative.
  • be polished and readable.
    • The content (i.e. quotes from the reading, your analysis, etc.) should be typed up and printed out, not handwritten.
    • The content should be laid out in a way that is easy to follow.
  • be designed in a way that fits the reading.
    • At a minimum, this means make design choices that don’t clash with the vibes of the reading.
    • You can also go further with this by theming your presentation.

Including the Original Reviews

For video reviews (i.e. TikTok, YouTube, etc.)

  • Include a screenshot of the video
  • Create a QR code so that your classmates can view the video on their phones (if so inclined)
  • Also print out the link to the video to place near the screenshot and QR code

For print reviews

  • Print out the review to attach to your presentation
  • For longer articles, you can staple the reviews to allow someone to flip through it on your display

Working with Classmates

If other students choose the same reading as you, you have the option to work together. Your displays would be set up near each other, so together they make an exhibit on that reading.

Each student still needs to analyze three reviews in connection to the reading. The content parts (reviews, quotes, analysis) are graded individually. The collaborative parts are graded as part of the presentation points, which cover how well the display is put together.

If you work together, you can’t use the same material as each other. For example, two students doing the same novel can’t both use the exact same New York Times review.
