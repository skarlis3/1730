If You Want to Pass

Read the books. Or listen to them. (Or don't, but the midterm will have opinions about your choices.)

As a literature class, the bulk of your out-of-class work for this course is reading. Most weeks will include literary readings ranging from several short stories to an entire novel in length. Plan accordingly — give yourself enough time each week to complete the readings before class.

Check the course calendar regularly so you know what's coming: some weeks are lighter, and some weeks will ask you to get through a novel. If you see a heavier reading week approaching, read ahead.

The midterm, final, paper, and final project are all based on the readings. There is no version of passing this class that skips them.

A note on audiobooks

Listening to a book is absolutely fine for this class. However, you will still need the text with you in class, in some format, since many class activities involve working directly with the text (locating passages, tracking keywords, close reading).

If you listen to audiobooks, choose a format that lets you search and navigate the text, such as read-along ebooks (Libby and the Kindle app both offer this on many titles) that give you narration synced to a searchable ebook. A standalone audio file with no text won't work for in-class activities.
